Artist Biography

AV.H.264 is a multimedia artist whose practice operates at the intersection of sculpture, moving image, and digital compression. Graduating with a Bachelor of Fine Arts, they are currently pursuing an MA at the Royal College of Art (RCA), London, where their research explores the friction between physical spatial boundaries and time-based digital frameworks.

Operating between London and West Sussex, they use physical structures and digital media to investigate how space is captured, compressed, and mediated. Their work has been included in selected group exhibitions and showcases, including the RCA Work-in-Progress show, and they are currently developing new work exploring the textures of domestic restriction and temporal hiatus.

Artist Statement

My practice considers multimedia art an act of spatial and temporal translation. Blending heavy physical media—such as cast metals, stone, and industrial timber—with time-based digital formats, I examine how physical boundaries and digital codecs dictate human perception. The handle av.h.264 serves as a conceptual nod to this framework: a recognition of how raw, monumental physical reality becomes compressed, formatted, and transmitted through digital channels.

Central to my work is the loop—both as a physical structural motif and a temporal cinematic device. I investigate the friction that occurs when permanent, unyielding industrial materials are paired with the transient, fleeting nature of moving images or soundscapes.

During my current institutional intermission, my practice scales down into intensive digital spatial programming, high-definition video editing, and the production of modular maquettes. These works function as self-contained micro-environments that critique the boundaries of the domestic sphere. Whether manifesting as a massive physical installation or a highly compressed digital render, the objective remains constant: to expose the unseen weights, loops, and holds that govern our material and digital realities.
